Finding Focus Through Sound

A musical sound can do something that some people don't know.

WRITING SOUND

Nobody needs another personal website in 2026. There are faster ways to be read, better-designed ways to be seen, and algorithms that would happily do the finding-an-audience part for me if I let them. I built this one anyway, slowly, on a Sunday, for reasons that took me longer to understand than the site took to build.

The honest answer is that writing for a platform and writing for a page feel like different acts, even when the words are similar. A platform is always, somewhere in the back of my head, asking whether this will travel. A page just sits there and asks whether it's true, and whether it's said as plainly as I can manage. I wanted more of the second question in my life and less of the first.

The audience of one

Early on, almost nobody read anything I put here, and I noticed something useful about that: the writing got better, not worse. Without an audience to perform for, the only reader I had to satisfy was the version of me who'd come back to this later, wondering what I'd actually thought. That's a harder reader to fool than a feed full of strangers. He remembers what I said last time.

Write for the person who has to live with what you decided, not the one who's scrolling past it.

That's still mostly who this is for. If other people find it useful along the way, genuinely - good. But I've tried to build a site that would still feel worth keeping even if the traffic never showed up: dated entries, a plain archive, nothing that depends on anyone else looking at it to justify its own existence.

What "in public" actually means here

Not everything. I edit. I cut the parts that are more confession than thought, and I sit on drafts until I'm sure I believe them, not just that I felt them on a Tuesday. Public, for me, doesn't mean unfiltered - it means accountable. Once something's dated and posted, I can't quietly pretend I never thought it. That constraint has done more for my writing than any amount of advice about audience or voice ever did.
