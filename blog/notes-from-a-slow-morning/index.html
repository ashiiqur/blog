Notes from a Slow Morning

On the mornings that refuse to be scheduled, and what they're for.

JOURNAL LIFE

There's a kind of morning that doesn't fit into the plan, and I've stopped fighting them. The kettle takes longer than it should. The light comes in at an angle that makes we want to sit in it instead of near it. Nothing gets crossed off a list, and for once that feels less like failure and more like information.

I used to treat these mornings as leaks — time slipping out of a schedule that was otherwise watertight. Now I think they're closer to a pressure valve. A week built entirely of efficient mornings is a week with no slack in it anywhere, and a system with no slack is a system one bad Tuesday away from breaking. The slow mornings are where the slack lives.

What actually happens in them

Not much, on paper. I read something unrelated to whatever I'm supposed to be working on. I notice the state of the plants. I let a thought run somewhere it wasn't scheduled to go, and sometimes it comes back with something useful, and sometimes it just comes back tired, which is its own kind of useful.

The essays I'm proudest of on this site didn't start at a desk. They started on mornings exactly like this — unplanned, unproductive by any normal measure, and quietly doing the thing that planned time can't do, which is make room for a sentence I didn't know I was building until it arrived.

Slack isn't the absence of a plan. It's the part of the plan that makes the rest of it survivable.

I'm not arguing for fewer plans. I like my plans. I just no longer treat the mornings that break them as a problem to solve before next week. Some of them are the point.
